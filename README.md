## PART A — ANSWERING FORMAT (how the team writes)

### A1. Skeleton used in almost every answer

```
Dear valued customer,                      ← or "Dear Valued Customer," / "Dear <Name>," if the client signed off with a name

[1] Opening line (pick one)
    • "Based on the limited information provided, we assume that:"   → list assumptions
    • "Please find our comments/responses below:"                   → for multi-question enquiries
    • "Thank you for your enquiry. Please find our comments below:"

[2] Assumptions (only those that would CHANGE the treatment — do not restate the scenario)
    • bullet list
    • "Please let us know if any of our assumptions are incorrect."

[3] Body — subtitled sections
    • Multi-question: number each part and repeat the client's question as the subtitle
      (e.g. "1. Do I need to pay the RM100 penalty or submit an appeal?")
    • Single issue: descriptive subtitles
      (e.g. "Stamp duty treatment", "Person liable to pay", "Whether the agreement needs to be submitted for stamping")
    • State the rule → cite the authority (Item / section / guideline para / media release / case) → apply to facts → conclusion
    • Use the wording of the Act / guideline, not own words or judgement (see A6)
    • Explain only the treatment the facts point to; for the alternative, state only that
      "the treatment would be different" (see A7)
    • Point form only for conditions, factors, steps; prose elsewhere
    • Tables for: rates by scenario, multi-tier contracts, timelines, Item 22(1)(a) vs (b)

[4] Recommendation / next step (where relevant)
    • "If the amount involved is material, we recommend that the agreement be submitted to IRBM for adjudication…"
    • "We recommend retaining the quotation, PO and invoice together with the agreement to support the exemption."

[5] Disclaimer (standard text — A4)

Thank you.
```

### A2. Writing conventions observed
| Convention | Practice in the bank |
|---|---|
| Hedging | "generally", "would generally", "may", "subject to the actual terms and legal effect of the agreement". Definitive answers avoided where the document hasn't been seen. |
| Substance over title | Nearly every answer anchors on: *stamp duty is imposed on the instrument, based on its substance and legal effect, not its title.* |
| Citing law | "Item 22(1)(a) of the First Schedule to the Stamp Act 1949"; "Section 47A of the Stamp Act 1949"; "paragraphs 2.3 and 2.5 of LHDN's Guidelines on Stamp Duty on Sale and Transfer Instruments for Movable Property (updated 29 December 2025)"; "HASiL's media release dated 7 August 2026"; "Question 3 of the HASiL–CTIM Tax Forum 2026: Session 4 – Self-Assessment of Stamp Duty". |
| Quoting | Quote the Schedule wording where it decides the case, e.g. Item 4 Exemption (a): *"for or relating to the sale of any goods, wares or merchandise other than a hire-purchase agreement."* |
| Abbreviations | Define once: Stamp Act 1949 ("SA 1949"), HASiL / LHDN / IRBM used interchangeably; STAMPS, e-Duti Setem (e-DS), STSDS. |
| Scope limits | State them plainly: *"Please note that our advice is limited to Malaysian stamp duty matters. We are unable to advise on the design or implementation of your Company's internal procurement and finance control procedures."* |
| Language | Reply in the client's language (17 answers are in Chinese; English otherwise). Keep Act/Item names in English inside Chinese answers. |
| Follow-ups | Open with "Thank you for your further clarification…" / "Please find our responses to your further queries below." and answer only the new points. |
| Internal resources | May point to taxPOD material, e.g. *"YYC taxPOD slide: Stamp Duty SVDP 2026 (updated 14 July 2026)"*. |
| Non-stamp-duty questions | Answered in the same format with their own subtitles (income tax, SST, WHT, e-Invoice, RPGT) — cite the Public Ruling / Act section. |

### A3. Standard phrases bank
- *"Under the Stamp Act 1949, stamp duty is imposed on chargeable instruments rather than the underlying transactions."*
- *"The supplier's signature, stamp or acknowledgement does not automatically make a PO subject to stamp duty… it is one factor in the overall assessment, rather than a decisive factor by itself."*
- *"Where a transaction contains both goods and service elements, the existence of ancillary services does not, by itself, change the essential character of the transaction into a service agreement."*
- *"Item 4 fixed duty of RM10 should not be treated as the default merely because the total contract value is not stated or cannot be determined upfront."*
- *"RM10 should not be treated as an optional duty merely because the customer requests stamping."*
- *"Based on the limited information provided, we are unable to provide a definitive conclusion without reviewing the actual [agreement]."*
- *"As the position is not free from doubt, we recommend submitting the [contract] for adjudication by the Collector of Stamp Duties (HASiL) to obtain certainty."*

### A4. Standard disclaimer (current wording — use verbatim)
> **Disclaimer**
> Kindly be informed that our response provided above is based on the limited details available and may not encompass the full context or complexities of the instrument in question. Please note that stamp duty treatment and the resulting payable amount vary significantly depending on the specific content, terms, and underlying conditions drafted within a contract.
>
> As the precise interpretation of contractual language is critical to determining tax liability, we strongly recommend consulting a qualified legal practitioner. A legal professional is the most appropriate authority to interpret the document's legal standing and provide a definitive decision regarding its specific stamp duty treatment. We may assist on a separate engagement where our team will liaise with you on the above matter.
>
> Thank you.

Variants: when the document **was** reviewed, drop "based on the limited details available"; for SVDP answers the disclaimer is conditional on eligibility (*"The above comments are based on the assumption that the instruments qualify under the PKPS 2026 Guideline…"*). Short factual answers (rates, deadlines) often omit the disclaimer.

### A6. Consultant stance — use technical wording, not own words or judgement
We answer as a consultant who **does not know the client's full company scenario**. The answer must not read as a judgement on the client's business or document. Describe the treatment in the terms used by the Stamp Act 1949, the First Schedule and HASiL guidelines, and tie every conclusion to the **instrument** and its **terms**, not to what we think the client does.

| Do not write (own words / judgement) | Write instead (technical wording) |
|---|---|
| "based on what it actually does" | "based on the **substance** of the instrument" |
| "what it is called" / "its name" | "its **title**" |
| "what the document really is" | "the **nature and legal effect** of the instrument" |
| "the deal" / "the arrangement you have" | "the **transaction** evidenced by the instrument" |
| "signed" / "the date you signed" | "**executed**" / "the **date of execution**" |
| "the paperwork" / "the contract stuff" | "the **instrument(s)**" / "the **related documents**" |
| "who pays" | "the **person liable** to pay the stamp duty under the Third Schedule" |
| "the main document" | "the **principal instrument**" (s.4(3)) |
| "the price" / "how much it's worth" | "the **consideration**" / "the **contract value**" / "the **market value**" |
| "your crane is stock" / "you're a trader" | "where the goods are **trading goods** of the seller" |
| "this looks like a service contract to us" | "where the instrument **constitutes** a service agreement" |
| "we think you're fine" / "you don't need to worry" | "the instrument **may qualify** for the exemption under …" |
| "it's basically an agreement" | "the instrument **may be regarded as** an agreement under Item 4" |
| "you should just stamp it to be safe" | "parties **may consider** submitting the instrument for **adjudication**" |
| "the tax office" / "the government" | "HASiL / LHDN / IRBM" / "the Collector of Stamp Duties" |

Rules of thumb:
- Phrase facts as **conditions**, not conclusions about the client: *"Where the agreement provides for…"*, *"If the instrument creates legally binding rights and obligations…"*, *"Provided that the service obligations are ancillary to the sale…"*.
- Assumptions are written as the conditions the treatment relies on — never as an assessment of the client's business.
- Do not add commentary, opinions on commercial choices, or reassurance. State the treatment, the authority and the condition.
- Use the same terms as the cited provision (e.g. "goods, wares or merchandise", "definite and certain period", "obligor", "conveyance on sale").

### A7. One-direction rule — explain only the treatment the facts point to
If the scenario points (even slightly) to **Direction A**, explain **only how the treatment under Direction A works** (Item, rate, person liable, conditions, stamping/endorsement). For anything that would take it out of Direction A, state **only that the treatment would be different** — do **not** explain Direction B (its Item, rate or computation).

Structure:
1. Assumption that places the instrument in Direction A.
2. Treatment under Direction A — Item, authority, rate/exemption, conditions, person liable, deadline.
3. One sentence: *"Where [condition of Direction A] is not met, the stamp duty treatment would be different."*
4. (Optional) Recommendation: review of the actual instrument / adjudication.

**Example — crane supply agreement (facts point to sale of goods):**
> *Stamp duty treatment*
> Under Item 4 of the First Schedule to the Stamp Act 1949, an agreement or memorandum of agreement is subject to stamp duty of RM10, except an agreement *"for or relating to the sale of any goods, wares or merchandise other than a hire-purchase agreement."* Where the crane constitutes trading goods of the seller and the design, installation and commissioning obligations are ancillary to the sale, the agreement may qualify for the exemption under paragraph (a) of the Exemptions to Item 4. Pursuant to HASiL's media release dated 7 August 2026, instruments under the "Exemptions" category of the First Schedule do not require stamping or endorsement.
>
> Where the agreement is not an agreement for or relating to the sale of goods based on the substance of the instrument, the stamp duty treatment would be different.

✗ **Do not** go on to explain Item 22(1)(a), the 0.5% / 0.1% rates or the lump-sum service-value issue in this answer.

**Other quick examples**
| Facts point to | Explain | Close with |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted quotation with detailed T&Cs for a tenancy | Item 49(a) treatment, rate by term, lessee liable, 30-day deadline | "Where the quotation does not constitute a binding lease or agreement for lease, the treatment would be different." |
| Fixed-term service agreement with ascertainable fee | Item 22(1)(a), 0.1% under P.U.(A) 428/2021, conditions, service recipient liable | "Where the total amount payable or the period cannot be ascertained, the treatment would be different." |
| Employment offer letter, wages ≤ RM3,000 | Item 4 exemption; no stamping/endorsement (7 Aug 2026 release) | "Where the monthly wages exceed RM3,000, the treatment would be different." |
| Sub-contract under a stamped private main contract | RM50 cap under P.U.(A) 428/2021 and the disclosure conditions | "Where the conditions are not met, the treatment would be different." |
| Instrument executed in 2024 (PKPS) | PKPS 2026 automatic penalty remission, pay by 31 Dec 2026 | "Where the instrument was executed outside 1 Jan 2023 – 31 Dec 2025, the treatment would be different." |

Only explain both directions where the client **expressly asks** about both (e.g. "what if it is treated as a service agreement?") — then answer the question asked, still in technical wording.

> **Note on Part B:** Part B sets out both directions for reference when drafting. When writing the answer, apply A6 and A7 — use only the part that matches the facts.

## PART B — KNOWLEDGE BASE

### 1. Core principles
| Principle | Authority |
|---|---|
| Duty is on the **instrument**, at the rates in the First Schedule. | s.4(1) SA 1949 |
| "Instrument" includes every written document; "writing" includes electronic records/transmissions. E-mails/e-signatures can be instruments. | s.2 |
| Treatment follows **content and legal effect, not the name** (e.g. a PO that functions as an agreement is chargeable). | First Schedule Guideline (30 Jun 2026) para 1.3, 2.2; Introduction to Stamp Duty (29 Dec 2025) |
| Applies even if signed by only one party, if it creates legal effect (e.g. bonds, statutory declarations). | Introduction to Stamp Duty |
| "Executed" = signed. Execution date = date the **last** party signs. | s.2; bank (offer-letter answers) |
| Several instruments completing **one** sale/lease/mortgage/settlement/exchange/partition: only the **principal instrument** bears full duty; the others RM10 each. Parties may choose which is principal. | s.4(3) |
| Who pays: the person named in the **Third Schedule**; otherwise the person who makes/prepares/executes the instrument. | s.33 |
| Unstamped chargeable instrument is **not admissible as evidence** in court; public officers may impound it. | s.52, s.51 |
| Minimum duty RM10. | s.36CB |
| Foreign-currency consideration: convert at the rate on the **date of the instrument** (if executed outside Malaysia, the bank applies the rate at stamping in Malaysia). | s.13(1) |
| Records (stamped instruments + stamp certificates) kept **7 years** from date duty paid. | s.35B |

### 2. Stamping deadlines & assessment
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Executed in Malaysia | Stamp within **30 days** of execution (s.47) |
| Executed outside Malaysia | Within **30 days after first received in Malaysia** (s.47) |
| Bills/cheques/notes made outside Malaysia | 30 days after first received in Malaysia (s.43) |
| **Self-assessment (STSDS)** — deemed assessment | BNDS submitted = assessment deemed raised that day (s.36(1)(a)); pay within **30 days of the deemed assessment** (s.36(2)) |
| **Formal assessment** | Pay within **14 days** of the notice of assessment or the period stated (s.40) |
| Platform | From 1 Jan 2026, all BNDS via **e-Duti Setem on MyTax** (mandatory; s.35A). TIN required for payer and parties. |
| STSDS phases | **Phase 1 – 1 Jan 2026:** tenancy/lease, security instruments, general stamping. **Phase 2 – 1 Jan 2027:** real property transfers not requiring JPPH valuation. **Phase 3 – 1 Jan 2028:** everything else. Phases 2–3 stay on formal assessment until then. |
| BNDS forms (Phase 1) | PDS 15 — Security (service agreements, licence agreements, equipment rental, loans); PDS 15 — Tenancy/Lease (tenancy, lease, novation, Form 15A); PDS 15 — General (statutory declarations, SPA of house, employment offer letters, constitution, insurance policy, immigration bond) |
| Exemption/remission claims | Always go to **formal assessment** even if the instrument is an STSDS category; endorsed by TPDS. |
| Stamp certificate | Instrument is "duly stamped" only after correct duty is paid **and** the certificate is attached (s.2, para 7(1)(c)). |

### 3. Late stamping penalty — s.47A
| When stamped | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Within 3 months after the deadline | **RM50 or 10%** of deficient duty, whichever higher (s.47A(1)(a)) |
| Beyond 3 months | **RM100 or 20%** of deficient duty, whichever higher (s.47A(1)(b)) |

Guideline illustrations (6 Nov 2025): RM10 duty stamped within 3 months → RM50; RM5,000 duty beyond 3 months → RM1,000; **exempt instrument submitted late** → RM100 (or RM50 within 3 months).
STSDS: lateness in submitting the BNDS and lateness in paying are **added together** to decide which band applies (e.g. 2m15d + 3 days = within 3 months → 10%; 2m15d + 2m20d = over 3 months → 20%).
Penalty appeals are only considered while the notice is **unpaid**. Extension-of-time requests must be made **before** the payment deadline.

Underpaid duty (s.36CA) and any s.47A late penalty still apply. Not available for formal-assessment categories or exemption/remission claims.

### 5. Special Voluntary Disclosure Programme — PKPS / SVDP Stamp Duty 2026
*(Operational guideline updated 29 June 2026 — supersedes the 1 Jan 2026 version)*
- **Qualifying instruments:** executed **1 Jan 2023 – 31 Dec 2025**.
- **Window:** stamping **and payment** between **1 Jan 2026 and 31 Dec 2026** (originally 30 June 2026; extended by MOF).
- **Effect:** 100% remission of s.47A penalty under s.47A(2); **automatic** — no appeal, no code to enter. The BNDS/notice may still show the penalty; it is removed at payment.
- Already stamped before 2026 but unpaid → still eligible if paid within the window. Already **paid** with penalty → not eligible (no refund).
- Instruments stamped under PKPS **will not be audited**; other instruments, and those stamped from **1 Jan 2027**, remain auditable.
- Not for fraud cases. Instruments executed **before 2023** or **in 2026** are outside the programme.

### 6. Exemptions, remissions & endorsement
- **General exemptions:** s.35 + First Schedule General Exemptions (e.g. instruments where the Government/Ruler is liable to pay). Local authority as payer: Stamp Duty (Exemption) (No. 16) Order 1986 [P.U.(A) 174/1986].
- **Item-specific exemptions:** Items 2, 4, 23, 24, 32, 49, 58, 59.
- **Ministerial exemption/remission:** s.80(1), 80(2) (gazetted), 80(1A) (case-specific letters).
- **Endorsement of exempt instruments:** s.37(2) / s.11.
- ⚠️ **HASiL media release, 7 August 2026** (as cited consistently in the bank from Aug 2026): instruments falling within the **"Exemptions" category of the First Schedule do not require stamping or endorsement** — e.g. Item 4 Exemption (a) sale-of-goods agreements, employment contracts ≤ RM3,000/month. Recommend submitting anyway where the exemption is doubtful (mixed goods/services) to get HASiL confirmation.

### 8. Service agreements (Item 22) — *Guideline dated 29 Sept 2026*
**Classification.** A service agreement is a **security instrument** because it creates an *obligation to pay money* — "security" here does **not** mean collateral. Covers construction, maintenance, security, consulting, advisory and any "work done for a customer". Dutiable value **includes SST**.

**Rate — Stamp Duty (Remission) Order 2021 [P.U.(A) 428/2021]** (instruments executed from 28 Dec 2018):
| Tier | Parties | Duty |
|---|---|---|
| First tier (private) | Principal service provider ↔ private party awarding the work | **0.1%** of contract value (duty above 0.1% remitted) |
| Government first tier | Principal provider ↔ Government/Ruler/State | **Exempt** (para 1, General Exemptions s.35); local authority: P.U.(A) 174/1986 |
| Sub-contract under a Government project | Principal provider ↔ sub-provider | **0.1%** |
| Subsequent tiers | Any further sub-contract | **Capped at RM50** |
| Small contracts | If full Item 22(1)(a) duty is **below RM50** | **Actual duty** — no remission (P.U.(A) 428/2021 para 2(3)) |
| Very small first-tier | 0.1% < RM10 | **RM10 minimum** (s.36CB) |

0.1% applies at **one tier only** in a multi-tier chain. Rounding: value rounded **up** to the next RM1,000 for the 0.5% computation; 0.1% computed on the actual amount (example: RM27,500,700 → RM27,501).

**Conditions for the RM50 cap** — the sub-contract must clearly state: (a) names of parties and date of the main contract; (b) subject matter; (c) that the main contract was **duly stamped** at the 0.1% (or exempt) level. Keep the main contract + LOA for audit.

**When P.U.(A) 428/2021 does not apply:**
- **Amount payable can't be determined** (e.g. RM115 per lorry load until done) → **Item 4, RM10**, payable by the executing party.
- **Period can't be determined** (e.g. panel clinic RM2,000/month, terminable on 3 months' notice) → **Item 22(1)(b)**, RM1 per RM100 → RM20.
- ⚠️ Bank nuance (Oct 2026): cost-plus or commission-based fees in a **fixed-term** agreement — HASiL SPK 2025 Q&A Q43/Q80 points to 22(1)(b) where consideration can't be determined, but 22(1)(b) refers to life/indefinite periods; flag the tension and recommend HASiL confirmation. **Do not default to RM10.**

**Who pays:** the **service recipient** (obligor — Third Schedule item 2), unless the contract shifts the cost commercially. The statutory liability doesn't move just because the provider refuses to stamp — the recipient can stamp it themselves.

**Common instruments treated as service agreements in the bank:** audit/professional engagement letters, KOL/affiliate agreements (if no employment relationship), advertising packages (whole bundled value incl. production/installation; mark-up is not separated), management fee agreements, software subscriptions/licences that operate as service agreements, LOAs/LOIs with substantive terms, equipment/car rental (Item 22 equipment lease), property management, dealer agreements.

### 9. Goods vs services, POs, quotations, invoices, LOAs
- **Sale of goods** → Item 4 **Exemption (a)** — no duty; per 7 Aug 2026 release, no stamping/endorsement needed.
- **"Goods" = trading stock of the seller only** — *Havi Logistics (M) Sdn Bhd v Pemungut Duti Setem* (Federal Court, 01(f)-5-02/2024(W)). Judge from the **seller's** perspective; buyer's use (fixed asset, cost of sales) is irrelevant (Movable Property Guideline, 29 Dec 2025, paras 2.2–2.3).
- Movable **asset** (non-trading) sold → ad valorem under s.21(1) / Item 32(a) (or 32(aa) for foreign buyers). If the sale agreement paid ad valorem, a separate transfer instrument is RM10. For trading goods: sale agreement exempt, but a **separate transfer instrument** (if any) is ad valorem.
- **Mixed goods + services:** look at the **predominant nature**. Ancillary installation/commissioning doesn't convert a supply contract — *Petronas Carigali Sdn Bhd v Pemungut Duti Setem* [2023] MLJU 639 (High Court; Item 4, not Item 22). Itemised service components may be assessed under Item 22 separately; lump-sum mixed contracts → recommend adjudication (risk of full value being treated as services). Optional separately-priced services (training, extended warranty, field reps) — review separately.
- **POs:** not chargeable merely because they exist; chargeable if they operate as a binding agreement with detailed contractual obligations (HASiL–CTIM Tax Forum 2026 Session 4, Q7, 11, 66). Supplier acknowledgement (signature, chop, e-mail) is one factor, not decisive. Value alone doesn't decide.
- **Quotations:** non-binding price offer → not chargeable. **Accepted** quotation with detailed T&Cs forming a binding contract → chargeable (Tax Forum 2026 Session 4, Q3). Acceptance may be electronic or by conduct.
- **Invoices:** billing documents only → generally not instruments.
- **Quotation + PO + acknowledgement:** read together; not automatically three instruments — decide whether they form one contractual arrangement (s.4(3) may apply).
- **Letter of Award / Acceptance:** with scope, period, payment terms, retention, DLP, or a clause making it binding until a formal contract → chargeable (Item 22 for services).

### 10. Employment contracts (most-asked topic — 70+ answers)
| Execution date | Duty | Penalty / action |
|---|---|---|
| **Before 1 Jan 2025** | **Exempt** (MOF/HASiL media statement, 6 June 2025) | Late penalties remitted; no need to stamp solely for compliance. Bank position: submit via e-Duti Setem for endorsement to obtain a free exemption certificate if the company wants it (see §14). |
| **1 Jan 2025 – 31 Dec 2025** | **RM10** per principal contract (> RM300/month) | Penalty 100% waived under **PKPS 2026 if stamped and paid by 31 Dec 2026** |
| **From 1 Jan 2026** | **Exempt if wages ≤ RM3,000/month**; **RM10** if > RM3,000 | Normal 30-day deadline and s.47A penalties. ≤ RM3,000: **no stamping or endorsement** required (7 Aug 2026 release). |

Legal basis for RM3,000: **Finance Act 2025 [Act 874], s.29(a)**, amending the Item 4 exemption (Budget 2026), effective 1 Jan 2026.

Other employment positions:
- Only the **principal** employment instrument bears RM10; offer letter + contract for one employment → s.4(3).
- Execution date = date the **last party signs** (e.g. employee signs on 28 Sep although started work in April → 28 Sep; 30 days from then).
- **Interns, part-timers, fixed-term, contract staff**: same rules where an employer–employee relationship exists (indicators: remuneration/allowance, fixed hours/location, assigned duties, reporting, supervision/control). Classification under Item 4.
- **No written contract → no instrument → no duty** (Employment Act 1955 still requires written contracts > 1 month).
- **Independent contractors** → service agreement, Item 22.
- **Renewals, increment letters, transfers, promotion letters**: each new instrument that varies rights is a separate instrument; reassess against the RM3,000 threshold at that point (HASiL FAQ on employment contracts, 3 July 2025, Q5, Q14, Q15).
- The RM3,000 exemption applies to **employment contracts only**, not service contracts.
- Director service agreements: treat as employment only where an employment relationship exists; otherwise Item 22.

### 11. Tenancy & leases (Item 49)
- Tenancy = ≤ 3 years; lease > 3 years must also be registered on **Form 15A** (NLC s.221), stamped RM10 under 49(e) if the lease agreement is duly stamped.
- Rates per §7. Duty on **average annual rent**; term counted from the lease commencement (may be before/after execution).
- **RM2,400 annual-rent exemption abolished from 1 Jan 2025** — all tenancies chargeable regardless of rent.
- **Option to renew** not yet exercised → duty on the initial term only; renewal/extension is a new instrument assessed on its rent and period.
- **Letter of offer** for tenancy: non-binding/preliminary → not the chargeable lease; if binding and a formal tenancy follows → s.4(3), one principal, the other RM10.
- Novation, sub-tenancy, sub-lease, car-park and kiosk occupancy agreements — chargeable. Equipment/vehicle rental falls under **Item 22**, not Item 49.
- Hotel/short-stay confirmation letters — review whether they create a lease of immovable property.

### 12. Loans & financing
- Loan agreements → Item 22(1)(a) at **0.5%** (RM5 per RM1,000); borrower pays. Example: RM100,000 intercompany loan → RM500.
- **Unsecured loan, repayable on demand or by single bullet, falling under 22(1)(b)** → **Stamp Duty (Remission) (No. 2) Order 2012 [P.U.(A) 258/2012]** — effective duty reduced to 0.1%. Remission is **not automatic**: must be genuinely indefinite, fully unsecured (no guarantee/security), on demand or bullet, and all related documents (drawdowns, rollovers) consistent. *(Verify exact remission wording against the gazette before quoting a figure.)*
- *Pemungut Duti Setem v AnnJoo Integrated Steel Sdn Bhd* (Court of Appeal): a maximum facility limit doesn't by itself make a facility a definite-term loan.
- Financing instruments listed in the guideline: facility agreements, Islamic financing (Murabahah, Tawarruq, MM), hire purchase (Item 22), charges (Form 16A), memorandum of deposit, letters of set-off, subordination, factoring.
- Stamp duty and legal fees on loan agreements are **not tax-deductible** (capital financing costs — PR 6/2006 para 6.8(i)).

### 13. Other recurring positions
| Topic | Position |
|---|---|
| **Supplementary agreement / addendum / VO** | Separate instrument. **Increase** in value → ad valorem on the **increment only**. **Decrease** or non-financial changes → **RM10**, produce the stamped original. No refund for a later reduction. |
| **Extension of time letters** | Extension of tenancy → generally ad valorem on extended rent/period; pure timeline extension of a fully stamped principal → argue RM10 (subject to LHDN). |
| **Duplicates/copies** | RM10 (Item 34); original stamped first (s.12). Additional copies of the stamp certificate can be requested. Subcontractor: obtain stamped copy + certificate from main contractor. |
| **Transfer of unlisted shares** | 32(b); value = higher of consideration and **NTA** per share (NTA = total assets − intangibles − liabilities). Preference shares: nominal + premium vs consideration. New company: consideration. SC-approved sales: SC-approved price. Form under s.105 CA 2016. Guideline 6 Nov 2019. |
| **Allotment of new shares** | Not a transfer — Item 32 ad valorem doesn't apply. Debt-to-equity conversion agreement → generally Item 4, RM10. |
| **Sale of business** | 32(a) on debts/receivables, FD, contract benefits, goodwill, other assets. Goodwill = consideration − (assets − liabilities); compare with stated goodwill, take higher. |
| **Real property transfer** | 32(a)/(aa)/(ab); higher of price or market value. E.g. RM500,000 house → RM1,000 + RM8,000 = RM9,000. |
| **Relief s.15 / s.15A** | Reconstruction/amalgamation; transfers between associated companies — no time limit (claim after paying). |
| **Refunds** | s.21(7) rescinded sale contract — 24 months; s.50B BNDS error — 24 months after BNDS; s.57 spoiled/unused instruments — 24 months (2 months where Land Office rejects transfer, proviso (aa)); s.58 overpaid / not chargeable — 24 months; s.39 court order and s.80 Ministerial — no time limit. Statutory declaration required. Only the Third-Schedule payer may claim. |
| **Objection / appeal** | Objection to Collector within **30 days** of the (additional) assessment (s.38A); appeal to **High Court within 21 days** of the decision, after paying the duty (s.39). Duty must be paid while objecting. |
| **Amendment requests** | For BNDS errors (not for exemption/relief claims). Non-financial edits → no new assessment; value changes → amended/additional assessment. |
| **Cancellation** | Allowed with reasons/documents (e.g. deed of revocation); refund only if a refund provision is satisfied. |
| **Audit** | General review (at HASiL office) or comprehensive review (at premises, all executed instruments). |
| **Compounded duty (s.9)** | Banks, brokers, insurers authorised to collect duty on cheques, policies, contract notes; remit by 14th of next month. |
| **RPGT** | Stamp duty borne by the disposer is a cost of transfer — RPGTA Sch. 2 para 6(1)(b). |
